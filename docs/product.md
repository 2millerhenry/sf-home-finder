# Product

## Register

product

## Users

One person comparing three San Francisco housing paths: a private room in a shared home, an entire studio/one-bedroom, or a 2–3 bedroom apartment split between the matching number of people. He checks the product briefly in the morning or evening, usually wants to make a fast decision, and should not need to understand scraping, YAML, schedulers, or source mechanics to use it.

## Product Purpose

Turn scattered housing listings into a trustworthy daily shortlist. Success means the best new options are obvious within seconds, promising listings can be saved for later, weak listings disappear from the active workflow, and every score is understandable enough to trust.

## Brand Personality

Calm, discerning, and quietly capable. The product should feel personal and native to a Mac, with the restraint and clarity associated with Apple software. Its voice is direct, plain, and reassuring without sounding corporate or cute.

## Anti-references

Use the scanning efficiency of a premium spreadsheet without looking like a raw default spreadsheet, generic analytics dashboard, admin panel, or colorful SaaS template. Avoid noisy toolbars, dense system status, decorative metrics, technical copy, novelty icons, and visual effects that compete with the listings.

## Design Principles

1. Start with the decision: show the strongest new homes before controls or system status.
2. Explain confidence: make the score, supporting evidence, unknowns, and concerns easy to understand.
3. Reduce each session to three actions: open, save, or dismiss.
4. Hide machinery until needed: source health, scans, and configuration are secondary details.
5. Preserve context: saved homes and personal notes should remain easy to find across future scans and rescoring.
6. Optimize for scanning: align comparable values in columns and show at least five recommendations in a standard laptop viewport.
7. Keep housing shapes explicit: a private room, studio, one-bedroom, shared-cost 2-bedroom, and shared-cost 3-bedroom must never be presented as interchangeable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Maintain strong text contrast, visible keyboard focus, comfortable targets, useful labels, reduced-motion support, and status communication that never depends on color alone. Use plain language and readable layouts that remain useful at narrow widths and browser zoom.
